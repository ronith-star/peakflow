# Figure 1 (supply_screen_map)

**Figure 1.** Map showing the 24 active planned data center sites in the Delaware River Basin and the municipal wastewater treatment plants whose effluent could supply their cooling water. Each site is shaded by the largest information technology (IT) load whose modelled peak-day cooling makeup the median effluent flow of the nearest eligible municipal plant within 10 miles could cover. 22 of 24 sites have such a plant able to cover at least 50 megawatts (MW) of IT load: 5 sites over 200 MW (solid) and 17 sites between 50 and 200 MW (half-tone). The remaining 2 sites (open) have no eligible plant within 10 miles. Dashed outlines mark the 10 sites whose location is approximate or uncertain or whose cited source could not be confirmed. Circle area is proportional to the median monthly effluent flow reported from July 2023 to June 2026 [epa_echo]; plants outside the basin are drawn at 40 percent opacity so that plants inside the basin read first. In the key, IT load coverable at peak is the largest IT load, in megawatts, whose peak-day makeup the nearest eligible plant's median flow covers. Dashed gray circles are 10-mile radii around each site. Numbers identify sites in the key below.

Demand is the calibrated hybrid cooling model at 13,286 gallons per day of peak-day makeup per MW of IT load, driven by hourly Trenton weather for 2005 to 2024 [noaa_isd; stull_2011] and calibrated to the reported Falls Township average cooling demand of 135,000 gallons per day and peak of 4.4 million gallons per day [falls_levittown_2026]. These rates are model-derived. Covered IT load does not depend on each site's own capacity, which 16 of 24 sites do not publish. Eligible plants are municipal major dischargers with at least 12 months of flow reports; industrial dischargers are excluded. The basin boundary is the union of hydrologic units 020401 and 020402 [usgs_wbd]; rivers, reservoirs and Delaware Bay are from NHDPlus High Resolution [usgs_nhdplus_hr]; state and county lines are the Census cartographic boundary files and city locations are TIGERweb place interior points [census_tiger]; relief is Natural Earth shaded relief [natural_earth]; site locations and sources are from the Data Center Proposal Tracker [trackdatacenters_2026]. The source line lists DRBC because the basin framing and review thresholds come from the Commission [drbc_admin_manual]. Albers equal-area conic projection, North American Datum of 1983, standard parallels 39 and 42 degrees north, central meridian 75.3 degrees west.
