# PeakFlow results

## Summary

Of the 24 active planned data center sites in the Delaware River Basin, 22 lie within 10 miles of a municipal treatment plant whose median effluent could supply cooling water [trackdatacenters_2026, epa_echo, peakflow_model]. The Falls Township campus reports cooling demand averaging 135,000 gal/day with a peak of 4.4 million gal/day, above the Commission's 100,000 gal/day review trigger [falls_levittown_2026, drbc_admin_manual]. Yet it required no review because it buys water from the Morrisville Municipal Authority. All 24 sites would be reviewed if they withdrew their own water, but 7 are reported to buy from a public or authority system and 15 have no published supplier [peakflow_model, drbc_datacenters_2026]. The 30-day averaging rule hides peak days only for hybrid designs of 7.53 to 23.6 MW [peakflow_model]. Under Monte Carlo uncertainty, the nearest plant covers the 90th-percentile peak day at 1 of 24 sites, or 6 of 24 if unstated sites are held at 100 MW [peakflow_model, shehabi_2024].

## Water supply of the planned sites

The published record names a water supplier for 9 of the 24 active planned sites [peakflow_model]. Seven are classed as public or authority supply. Six of these rest on a statement naming the supplier [falls_keystone_2026, project_washington_whyy_2026, east_whiteland_inquirer_2026, smithfield_langan_2026, limerick_laurel_chestnuthill_2026, kline_fox56_2026]. The seventh, the Allen Township site, rests on a conditional reference to the local authority's service area [allen_pc_minutes_2026]. Two are classed as self-supplied. Project Gold in Clifton and Covington Townships proposes an on-site well system for domestic and humidification use and trucked water for cooling [project_gold_wvia_2026]. The Slate Belt campus in Upper Mount Bethel Township is the other self-supplied site. The developer's earlier plan for that site drew on 10 on-site wells [river_pointe_wfmz_2023]. No opened source names a supplier for the remaining 15 sites, which are classed as unknown [peakflow_model]. Stated supply is not always secured. At Smithfield Gateway, the named authority declined a will-serve letter [smithfield_langan_2026]. At East Whiteland, work is halted pending Aqua's confirmation of capacity [east_whiteland_inquirer_2026].

Applied to each site at its stated or assumed IT load, the calibrated hybrid puts every site above the review trigger on its maximum 30-day average [peakflow_model, drbc_admin_manual]. The smallest load in the list, the assumed 100 MW, reaches 424,437 gal/day [peakflow_model, drbc_admin_manual]. So all 24 would be reviewed if self-supplied, and none falls in the averaging band [peakflow_model, drbc_admin_manual]. The trailing 30-day average exceeds 100,000 gal/day on 57 to 128 days per valid window-year across the sites [peakflow_model]. Sixteen of the 24 loads are the assumed 100 MW rather than a stated capacity [trackdatacenters_2026]. Of the 24 sites that would be reviewed if self-supplied, the 7 that purchase from a public or authority system would not themselves be reviewed, 2 are reported as self-supplied, and the supply of 15 is unknown [peakflow_model, drbc_datacenters_2026].

## Cooling model and Falls calibration

The hybrid model was calibrated to the Falls Township filing: an annual average cooling demand of 135,000 gal/day, a peak day of 4.4 million gal/day and water cooling in 6 percent of annual hours [falls_levittown_2026, falls_herald_2026]. The separate potable demand of 19,000 gal/day is not used in the fit [falls_levittown_2026]. Every source describes 4.4 million gal/day as a daily withdrawal, so the primary fit reads it as the largest calendar-day makeup, giving a switchover wet-bulb temperature of 22.4 °C, a part-load exponent of 0.429 and 331 MW of IT load [peakflow_model, noaa_isd, stull_2011]. Reading it instead as the maximum hourly rate over a full day gives 293 MW and 0.355, reported as a sensitivity [peakflow_model]. Three parameters are fitted to three targets, so the fit is exactly determined. Its near-zero residuals show that a solution exists and are not validation [peakflow_model]. IT capacity was inferred from the fit, not sourced. An unverified 253 MW figure is carried only as a sensitivity [cleanview_keystone_2026]. With Amazon's statement that water cooling runs less than 2 percent of the year, the three targets cannot be met simultaneously: matching the average and wet share raises the modeled peak day to 7.18 million gal/day, and matching the peak and wet share caps the average near 82,000 gal/day [amazon_falls_campus_2026, peakflow_model]. The reported peak is about 33 times the average [falls_levittown_2026].

The implied site water use effectiveness, annual site water per unit of IT energy [greengrid_wue_2011], is 0.0643 L/kWh on the primary fit, 0.0727 on the design-rate reading, 0.0842 at 253 MW and 0.0442 for the 2 percent case using its achieved average [peakflow_model, cleanview_keystone_2026]. The primary value is about 18 percent of the United States average of 0.36 L/kWh, about 24 percent of Microsoft's fleet value of 0.27, and below the 0.1 to 0.3 L/kWh reported for economizer systems with adiabatic cooling [shehabi_2024, microsoft_datacenters_2025]. A plausible annual average can therefore coexist with a large peak day.

## Falls Township daily makeup and river flow

The calibrated hybrid concentrates its water use in a few summer days. Modeled makeup is zero on 6,238 of 7,204 valid local days from 2005 to 2024 (86.6 percent), and the modeled maximum of 4.4 million gal/day, on 22 July 2011, is 32.6 times the modeled mean of 135,000 gal/day [peakflow_model, falls_levittown_2026, falls_herald_2026]. Single-day makeup exceeds 100,000 gal on 12.0 percent of valid days (866 days) and exceeds the reported average of 135,000 gal/day on 11.7 percent (840 days) [peakflow_model, falls_levittown_2026].

The top 5 percent of days by makeup, 361 days at 1.12 million gal/day or more, do not coincide strongly with low river flow: 23.8 percent of them (86 days) fall below the same-day 25th percentile of Trenton flow for that calendar day, close to the 25 percent expected by chance, and none falls below the full-record 7Q10 of 1,795 cfs, because no daily flow from 2005 to 2024 was lower than 2,370 cfs [peakflow_model, usgs_nwis_01463500]. The Commission's basinwide drought table lists no warning or emergency stage between 2005 and 2024 [drbc_drought_page]. The page text describes three such periods: the Lower Basin drought warning of 24 September to 31 October 2010 and the water supply emergencies of November 2016 to January 2017 and December 2024 to June 2025 [drbc_drought_page]. Only the 2010 warning falls in the cooling season, and it contains one top 5 percent day, 30 September 2010 [drbc_drought_page, peakflow_model].

## Regulatory blind spot

DRBC review is triggered when the daily average gross withdrawal over any 30 consecutive days exceeds 100,000 gallons [drbc_admin_manual]. The first mechanism, averaging, hides peak days only in a narrow band. No configuration in the 12-cell grid stays below the trigger on every 30-day average while exceeding 100,000 gal on a peak day, and the calibrated hybrid does so only between 7.53 and 23.6 MW [peakflow_model]. Against the annual mean of the 30-day average, 3 of 12 would qualify [peakflow_model]. The calibrated hybrid exceeds the trigger on a 30-day average on 24.8, 56.8, 88.4 and 105 days per year at 50, 100, 200 and 400 MW [peakflow_model]. The second mechanism is larger. Because the thresholds apply to a project's own withdrawal, a site that buys water from an existing public or authority system is not reviewed at any size [drbc_admin_manual, drbc_datacenters_2026]. Eight of the 12 configurations would be reviewed if self-supplied, and none if they purchase water [peakflow_model]. Falls exceeds the trigger on average, at 135,000 gal/day, with a modeled maximum 30-day average of 1.41 million gal/day [falls_levittown_2026, peakflow_model]. It required no Commission review because the Morrisville Municipal Authority supplies it. On hybrid days above 100,000 gal, Trenton flow was below its day-of-year 25th percentile on 23.9 to 25.3 percent of days and never below the 7Q10 of 1,795 cfs [usgs_nwis_01463500, peakflow_model].

## Peak-day uncertainty

A Monte Carlo analysis of 10,000 draws with seed 20260925 propagated three sources of uncertainty into each site's peak-day makeup [peakflow_model]. Cooling architecture was drawn from LBNL's 2023 hyperscale cooling-system shares, grouped into evaporative tower 0.016, hybrid 0.914 and air-cooled chiller 0.070 [shehabi_2024] (p. 39). Annual site water usage effectiveness was drawn from LBNL's large-scale simulated ranges, 1.72 to 2.78 L/kWh for the waterside economizer with water-cooled chiller and 0 to 1.56 L/kWh for the airside economizer with adiabatic cooling [shehabi_2024] (p. 46). Power usage effectiveness was drawn uniformly on 1.15 to 1.35 [shehabi_2024] (p. 48). Annual values were converted to a peak day with the calibrated maximum-day to mean-day ratio, 1.0 for the evaporative tower and 32.6 for the hybrid, with hybrid days capped at full evaporation [peakflow_model]. The cap binds in 92 percent of hybrid draws, which narrows the per-megawatt spread [peakflow_model]. The 16 sites without a stated capacity drew their IT load from the 8 stated values, 242 to 1,250 MW [trackdatacenters_2026, peakflow_model].

Per megawatt of IT load, the draws give a median peak day of 15,445 gal/day and a 90th percentile of 16,615 gal/day, against 13,286 gal/day in the deterministic screen [peakflow_model]. The nearest plant's median flow covers the 90th-percentile peak day at 1 of 24 sites, the Allentown Warehouse Conversion Data Center [peakflow_model, epa_echo]. The 90th percentile exceeds the plant's median flow at the other 21 of the 22 sites that have an eligible plant [peakflow_model, epa_echo]. A peak-day cap written at the 90th percentile therefore moves 6 of the 7 deterministically matchable sites to partial: Comcast Moorestown, Joint Base McGuire-Dix-Lakehurst, AWS Keystone Trade Center Campus, Sellersville and West Rockhill, NorthPoint Spring Mountain and Conshohocken [peakflow_model]. Most of this change comes from the capacity draws: with unstated sites held at the screen's 100 MW, the 90th percentile is covered at 6 of 24 sites [peakflow_model]. Only Joint Base McGuire-Dix-Lakehurst changes class. The count covered at the 90th percentile stays at 1 of 24 under LBNL's midsize and colocation weights, under assumed weights of 0.3, 0.5 and 0.2, under the range for a dry cooler with adiabatic assist, and under a conversion that re-solves the hybrid switchover temperature for each drawn water usage effectiveness [peakflow_model, shehabi_2024].

For the Falls Township campus, 2,000 draws of IT load (253 to 331 MW), power usage effectiveness (1.15 to 1.35) and part-load exponent (0.355 to 0.429), each run over one randomly chosen weather year from 2005 to 2024, give a median annual maximum day of 2.88 million gal/day, with a 10th to 90th percentile range of 2.04 to 3.81 million gal/day [peakflow_model, cleanview_keystone_2026, shehabi_2024, noaa_isd]. Every draw exceeds the 100,000 gal/day trigger on its largest day, and 86.6 percent of trace-days have zero makeup [peakflow_model, drbc_admin_manual].

## Water and energy

The choice of cooling system trades water against electricity, which is the first reason developers resist reuse-first terms. At Trenton weather an evaporative tower uses about 5,110,000 gallons per MW-year of IT load and the calibrated hybrid about 149,000 gallons, while an air-cooled chiller uses no evaporative makeup [peakflow_model]. The air-cooled chiller pays for this in energy: its median non-IT load in LBNL Figure 4.4 corresponds to a PUE of 1.83, or 7.25 million kWh per MW-year, against a PUE of 1.20 (1.74 million kWh) for a large-scale dry cooler with adiabatic assist and 1.12 (1.03 million kWh) for a large-scale waterside economizer with a cooling tower [shehabi_2024, peakflow_model]. Part of that gap reflects facility size, because LBNL models the air-cooled chiller only for midsize facilities. Within the midsize class, the gap between air-cooled and water-cooled chillers is 2.69 million kWh per MW-year, compared with 5.51 million kWh between the air-cooled chiller and the hybrid [shehabi_2024, peakflow_model]. LBNL states the same tradeoff directly: evaporative systems are generally more energy efficient, and air-cooled chillers use no water but more energy [shehabi_2024] (p. 45).

The second reason is treatment. Secondary municipal effluent carries biodegradable organic matter, ammonia, carbonate and phosphate that drive biofouling, corrosion and scaling. A Department of Energy study of power plant cooling found tertiary treatment essential for use in recirculating systems [dzombak_2012] (PDF p. 3). That study estimated tertiary-treated makeup at $0.91 to $1.32 per thousand gallons in 2009 dollars, above $0.74 for river withdrawal and treatment and below $2.95 for city water, with the DRBC raw-water rate at $0.08 per thousand gallons in the same comparison [dzombak_2012] (PDF pp. 15 and 190). The third reason is approval. The basin's data centers buy water from public systems and none has applied to the Commission [drbc_datacenters_2026]. A reuse contract replaces a supply that needs no Commission review with a new treatment and delivery arrangement whose state approvals were not reviewed here.

Reclaimed water changes the tradeoff by lowering the scarcity cost of the water axis rather than moving any design along it. An evaporative tower or the calibrated hybrid supplied from effluent keeps its energy advantage over the air-cooled chiller, 6.22 million and 5.51 million kWh per MW-year [peakflow_model]. It draws nothing from potable or river supply. On the two quantities a basin regulator weighs, fresh-water withdrawal and electricity, the evaporative tower and the calibrated hybrid therefore dominate the air-cooled design. This holds if the energy for tertiary treatment and delivery, which is not counted here, stays below that margin. The airside economizer with adiabatic cooling reaches a lower median PUE, 1.14 with a water-cooled chiller and 1.17 with an air-cooled chiller [shehabi_2024]. It uses 970,000 and 463,000 gallons per MW-year [peakflow_model]. These designs are also candidates for effluent supply, although the reported water use of the air-cooled variant, 0.1 to 0.3 L/kWh, is well above LBNL's simulated value [shehabi_2024] (p. 47).

## Cycles of concentration

No matched plant reports silica or chloride in its DMRs [epa_echo]. The Water Quality Portal was not reachable from this build [wqp_status.json]. Because of this, all 19 matched plants, including Trenton Sewer Utility and the Falls same-state option Morrisville Borough STP, use literature secondary-effluent calcium (60 mg/L), alkalinity (131 mg/L as CaCO3) and chloride (130 mg/L) [vidic_2009] (PDF p. 29). These plants are marked assumed [cycles_by_plant.csv]. Each plant's own DMR total dissolved solids and total phosphorus are used where reported [epa_echo]. Where dissolved solids are reported, chloride is estimated using a ratio of 0.34 times that value, which is the median ratio at the 10 eligible plants that report both values [cycles_summary.json].

Municipal effluent silica is taken to track the source water rather than a measured result. The Dogra review also states this assumption [dogra_2023] (PDF p. 14). The silica value is set to the surface-water median of 14 mg/L [hem_1985] (p. 73). This falls within a secondary-effluent range of 8.3 to 50 mg/L [vidic_2009] (PDF p. 30).

Allowable cycles are the minimum over a silica limit of 150 mg/L as SiO2 [midkiff_1977, PDF p. 3] [dogra_2023, PDF p. 5], a chloride level of 1060 mg/L that a tower of low carbon steel, stainless steel and copper alloy metallurgy carried without localized corrosion [geiger_1993] (PDF pp. 2, 4, 7), an orthophosphate level of 15 mg/L as PO4 held without calcium phosphate precipitation [geiger_1993] (PDF p. 5), and a Langelier saturation index of 2.5 [geiger_1993] (PDF p. 3). The chloride and phosphate values are demonstrated operating levels under a proprietary inhibitor program, not design limits. The index is computed as pH minus the calcite saturation pH from Hem's equilibrium constants and Debye-Hueckel activities at 35 C bulk water [hem_1985] (pp. 15, 19, 253). The concentrated water's pH is taken as its equilibrium with atmospheric carbon dioxide [hem_1985] (p. 92), capped at 8.5 [vidic_2009] (PDF p. 167).

On this basis the allowable cycles range from 0.9 to 7.0 with a median of 3.1, and 12 of the 19 plants fall below the model's 4 cycles [cycles_summary.json, peakflow_model]. Phosphate binds at 14 plants, the saturation index at 4 and chloride at 1 [cycles_by_plant.csv]. Trenton reaches 1.0 cycles and Morrisville 1.4 cycles on phosphate [cycles_by_plant.csv]. Catasauqua's effluent phosphate already exceeds the limit [cycles_by_plant.csv]. Phosphorus removal or pretreatment would be a condition of any term sheet. Without the phosphate constraint the allowable cycles range from 4.6 to 7.0 (Morrisville 4.6, Trenton 5.9), set by the saturation index at 15 plants and chloride at 4 [cycles_summary.json, cycles_by_plant.csv]. Defining the implied minimum return flow as blowdown divided by makeup, 1/C, the model's 4 cycles implies 25 percent [peakflow_model], the phosphate-limited median is 32 percent, and the range without the phosphate constraint is 14 to 22 percent [cycles_summary.json]. The saturation-index result depends on the pH cap: at the uncapped equilibrium pH of 9.3 to 9.4 the index limit is reached at 2.4 to 2.7 cycles [cycles_summary.json], so an acid or inhibitor program is implicit in every case above 3 cycles.

## Reproducibility and sources

Every raw file listed in data/raw/MANIFEST.md is downloaded by `make data`. This includes the DRBC rule and data-centers pages, the four Falls Township press pages, the LBNL 2024 report and the Natural Earth populated places archive. `fetch --verify` fails unless all 357 manifest rows exist with the recorded SHA-256 [peakflow_model]. The live check of site source URLs runs only in `make urls`, so `make map` is offline and repeatable [peakflow_model]. Six mapped sites cite a replacement for a source that is bot-blocked or expired. Three are Wayback Machine snapshots [internet_archive_wayback]. Three are alternate outlets. These include the Limerick Township hearings page for the Linfield site [limerick_cu_hearings_2026] and a WFMZ report for the Upper Mount Bethel campus [wfmz_slate_belt_2026]. The DataOne Vineland and Linfield sites are held unverified until their sources are reconfirmed. This leaves 21 of 24 mapped sites with a confirmed source and 10 of 24 drawn with a dashed outline [trackdatacenters_2026, peakflow_model]. The 39 planned-site records not drawn on the map, 11 inactive, 5 not data center records and 23 near-miss records outside the basin, are logged in results/exclusions.csv [trackdatacenters_2026].

## Assumptions

1. Power usage effectiveness is 1.2 [shehabi_2024]. Cycles of concentration are 4, an assumption pending the water-quality limits [peakflow_model].
2. Evaporation removes 2.43 MJ/kg of latent heat [peakflow_model]. Drift is neglected. Makeup is treated as gross withdrawal.
3. Trenton hourly weather for 2005 to 2024, with Stull wet-bulb temperature, is applied to every site [noaa_isd, stull_2011].
4. Stated campus power is divided by 1.2 to give IT load, and sites without stated capacity are assigned 100 MW [peakflow_model].
5. Eligible plants are municipal majors with at least 12 months of flow reports from July 2023 to June 2026, and supply is the median monthly flow of the nearest plant within 10 miles [epa_echo].
6. Where ECHO reports no design flow, screening uses actual average flow, then the past-year average, then the DMR median, and 34 of 258 retained permittees use a fallback [epa_echo].
7. The basin is hydrologic units 020401 and 020402 [usgs_wbd].

## Completeness and caveats

Trenton weather lacks 0.920 percent of hours and Philadelphia lacks 0.107 percent, and the Trenton record for 2005 alone lacks 10.2 percent [noaa_isd]. The gauge record lacks 2 of 41,632 days [usgs_nwis_01463500]. The 7Q10 includes 391 provisional days [usgs_nwis_01463500]. Approved data alone give 1,787 cfs [peakflow_model]. Of 258 retained permittees, 39 report no flow and 9 are in Delaware, including Wilmington at 63.3 million gal/day [epa_echo, peakflow_model]. No site class changes occurred. Chloride is reported by 14 permittees and silica by none, so cooling-water quality is not yet screened [epa_echo]. Monthly DMR flows understate daily variability, so coverage of a peak day is approximate [epa_echo]. No gap was interpolated. Ten of 24 sites are unverified: 3 have a source that could not be confirmed, 8 have an approximate or uncertain location, and 1 site is in both groups [trackdatacenters_2026]. All 10 are among the 22 sites within 10 miles of an eligible plant [peakflow_model]. Location is exact for 16 sites, approximate for 6 and uncertain for 2 [trackdatacenters_2026]. Eleven inactive sites and 23 sites outside the basin are excluded, among them New Castle Data Center at 0.2 km and Nebius Butler Township, 1,200 MW, at 1.1 km, and Project Gold lies 1.95 km inside the divide [trackdatacenters_2026]. The threshold reading rests on 18 CFR 401.35(a) as quoted in sources.md [drbc_admin_manual].
